The mechanism

Fully homomorphic encryption lets a contract operate on values it never sees. Everything below happens on Sepolia, in public, without the number ever existing in the clear on-chain.

Mint to reveal, end to end

  1. 01

    The draw

    FHE.randEuint16() asks the coprocessor for randomness that is encrypted from the instant it exists. It is folded into 1–100 with rem and add — all on ciphertext. No plaintext randomness appears in calldata, in an event, or in storage.

  2. 02

    The seal

    Storage gets a 32-byte handle, not a value. FHE.allowThis lets the contract keep using it; FHE.allow names you. The ACL is the whole security model — possession of a handle grants nothing.

  3. 03

    The look

    Your browser makes a throwaway keypair and signs an EIP-712 request scoped to this contract. The relayer checks the on-chain ACL, then re-encrypts the value to that keypair. The plaintext exists only in your tab.

  4. 04

    The handover

    OpenZeppelin's _update hook fires on every transfer, extending the ACL to the incoming holder in the same transaction. Ownership was always public. The essence follows the token, not the minter.

Worth knowing

Grants are permanent

FHEVM has no revoke. A past holder keeps the ability to decrypt the handle they held, so “only the current holder” holds against anyone who never held it. Rotating the secret on transfer would mean redrawing the trait — a different product.

The draw has a small bias

randEuint8(upperBound) only takes powers of two, so the contract draws 16 bits and reduces mod 100. The bias is under 0.06% — fine for a cosmetic trait, not for anything adversarial.
